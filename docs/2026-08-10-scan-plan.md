# Scan plan — collect findings widely, then triage them together

**Written 2026-08-10, before the scan, so the goal survives context compaction.**

---

## ★ THE GOAL — what we are actually trying to achieve

Steven's goal for APG, unchanged since it was set:

> when it becomes actually useful and would give us gains in knowledge and token
> usage — so we want it to make our agents better at their work and/or make them
> consume less tokens.

**The one number that judges this release:** sc-manager, asked to name a single
time a graph verb changed what they did, answered **zero** — and then retracted
their own excuse after testing, because the graph *was* reachable and trusted and
they still did not reach for it.

So the scan is not looking for features. It is looking for **why a competent agent
with a working graph does not use it**, and for **outputs that are confidently
wrong**, because those are the two things that decide whether the tool is worth
its cost.

### The finding that shapes what counts as valuable

Measured, from counterfactuals against errors an agent had published:

> Every behaviour change came from a field that CONTRADICTED my confidence. Not
> one came from a field that gave me more information.

⇒ **More data surface buys cheaper-same. More contradiction surface buys behaviour
change.** A finding that proposes new information is worth less than one that
proposes a way to catch a wrong belief. Weight the scan accordingly.

### The corollary that shapes what counts as a fix

> A cheaper useless field is worse than an expensive one, because cost was the one
> honest pressure to delete it.

⇒ **Prefer deletion to shrinking.** "Make it optional" is not a weaker deletion —
for a WRONG field it is worse, and for a DUPLICATE it converts a redundancy bug
into an install-dependent consistency bug.

## Success criteria for the scan itself

1. **Volume, then survival.** Collect widely and cheaply; a finding costs little,
   a wrong fix costs a lot. Then every finding must survive triage by at least one
   agent who did not produce it.
2. **Every finding carries evidence and scope.** "Not found" means "not found
   where I looked" — say where.
3. **Every ratio carries its denominator in its name**, not in an adjacent note.
4. **Observation separated from diagnosis**, with which one was verified stated.
   Three times this month an observation was right and its diagnosis wrong; the
   reports were actionable *because* the two were labelled apart.
5. **A well-stated null is a result.** A dead-end scope reported clearly is worth
   more than a padded list.

## Scan scopes, with what each is looking for

1. **Confidently-wrong outputs.** Any verb that can return a plausible answer
   that is false. Highest value; this is the class that has produced every
   behaviour change we can measure.
2. **Success envelopes that do not attest their corpus.** *(named by
   graph-senior-dev from four separate upstream projects plus our own)* — "indexed
   successfully" compatible with missing files; "complete" with 0 files; lock
   failure reporting up-to-date.
3. **Per-language quality gaps.** Measured: C++ 1.4% / 3.8%, JS 14.6%, PHP 105.3%
   trust-relevant unresolved. Are these extractor gaps or classifier gaps? They
   need different fixes.
4. **Fields nobody reads** — deletion candidates, re-measured **per language**,
   because a field is 11 tokens on JS and 649 on C++.
5. **Tests that cannot fail.** Three found this week that asserted the buggy
   invariant they were written to catch.
6. **Dead surface.** 42 verbs, 17 listed. Are the other 25 reachable, used,
   duplicated?
7. **Borrowable contradiction mechanisms** from reference repos ✅ delivered.

## The triage, after collection

**This is the part Steven asked for explicitly and it does not get skipped.**

Every finding goes through, with all three agents present in the thread:

1. **Is the observation real?** Reproduced by someone who did not report it.
2. **Is the diagnosis right?** Separately — three times this month it was not.
3. **Contradiction or data?** Data-surface findings are deprioritised by default
   and must argue their way up.
4. **Fix, delete, or refactor?** Deletion is the default for anything unread;
   "make it configurable" is rejected unless it survives the WRONG/DUPLICATE/
   UNREAD-BUT-CORRECT categorisation.
5. **What does it cost, per language?** Never one repo's numbers.
6. **Survives?** → the new plan. **Dies?** → recorded with the reason, so it is not
   re-proposed.

⚠ **Findings that die must be written down with why.** This session has already
re-litigated the tool-split question twice because the reasoning was in a thread
rather than a document.
