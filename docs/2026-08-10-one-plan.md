**Instance 1 — sc-manager.** Asked to name a single time a graph verb changed what

**Instance 2 — sc-manager again, different week, unprompted.** Their lanes spent the

★ It is not reassurance, and ef-manager's sharper statement is the one that goes in
slot 1: **they did not merely fail to reach for it — they reached for something better

is that the trust/evidence layer is the product and the code-intelligence layer has not
yet earned its place. Their hand-rolled per-form positive controls fired. My provenance
tiers did not — same day, same axis, see section 2.

### What this workstream is NOT

Not more fields, not more verbs, not better ranking. Those were all measured and
rejected (section 9). The finding is not "the output is insufficient."

### What it is

1. **Ask the two managers the question directly, as a question about their work and
   not about my tool:** at the moment you chose `git cat-file` over `graph_callers`,
   what did you expect the graph to do that `git cat-file` would not, and what did you
   expect it to cost? Their *anticipations* are the data. A retrospective "I forgot"
   is not.
2. **The cold-orientation experiment** (section 8) answers the other half: whether a
   reader with NO history reaches for the verb surface at all, or bypasses it for
   grep. ef-manager pre-registered that the bypass outcome is the headline if it
   happens, and it is the outcome that would make section 1 the whole roadmap.
3. **Deliverable:** a written answer to "what would have to be true for a competent
   agent to reach for this first," ranked, with the items we can act on separated
   from the ones we cannot. If nothing in sections 2–7 addresses it, the plan says so
   in those words.

⚠ **The honest possibility this workstream must be allowed to reach:** that the tool
is good and is still not worth reaching for, because a self-checking substitute is
cheaper than a tool you must first decide whether to trust. Section 2 is the price of
being trusted; section 1 is whether being trusted is enough.

## 3 — The apparatus that was supposed to catch section 2  ·  owner: graph-senior-dev-hermes

Audited 2026-08-10. Denominators are the auditor's, stated in their own names.

- **68 of 1,593 declared cases invoke ZERO production behaviour.** They read
  implementation files and assert regexes, token order, source absence — and in one
  case a production **comment**. All 68 stay green if the named behaviour becomes
  unreachable while the source spelling survives.
- **2 further cases pass with no assertion at all** on live paths
  (`brief/generator.test.js` READ filter; the EXPORTS-conditional exclusion).
- **23 cases use `if (...) return` instead of skip semantics.** 4 are silent green
  no-ops on this win32 host; up to 19 would be on POSIX. They report PASS.

**Proof this is not hygiene:** replacing ONE of the 68 with a behavioural test found
2.3 in three minutes — in the very file whose grep-test it replaced.

**And the false-positive half, found the same hour:** the only suite failure after
2.3's fix was *another* source-grep test, which broke because a line was **reflowed**
while the behaviour it guarded got stricter.

★ **The two failure modes are ONE cause, not two** (ef-manager): it asserts TEXT, and
text varies for reasons uncorrelated with behaviour — *in both directions*. So this is
not a weak test. **"A test with zero correlation to correctness is a random number
generator attached to your attention"**, and it costs the same attention whichever way
it fires.

### ★★ The three-layer pattern — a false positive DELETES a warning

Three independent instances in one day, which is a pattern claim rather than an
anecdote:

| layer | the false positive | the warning it deleted |
|---|---|---|
| the **field** | `tests_adjacent` listing a test on a `vec3` edge | suppressed `no_test_coverage` — the safety axis reported SAFE |
| the **tier** | `import_linked` claimed on a non-import edge | cleared `tests_unverified_for_symbol` on a borrowed warrant |
| the **test** | 68 cases that cannot fail for the right reason | suppress the no-coverage signal for the suite itself — they make it GREEN |

**Specimen to lead with:** `expect(src).toMatch(/… \/\/ and the file must not be
listed/)` — an assertion on a production comment, written the same day as the fix it
was meant to guard. Delete the predicate, keep the comment, green.

⇒ This is why source-grep tests are **worse than no tests**: no test claims nothing; a
test that cannot fail claims coverage.

### Work
1. **Classify, do not delete blindly.** Structural/source-contract tests get their own
   class. The green headline carries **behavioural / source-contract / skipped** as
   separate denominators. A single "1,667 pass" overstates behavioural confidence.
2. Replace the highest-risk contradiction guards with real fixtures and
   **deletion-sensitive** assertions.
3. Make zero-assert paths fail.
4. Convert platform `return`s to real skips with visible counts.
5. ★ **Add the incremental-vs-force canonical equivalence oracle.** None exists. The
   suite can be green while incremental indexing converges to a different graph than a
   clean rebuild. Minimum to be meaningful: add/change/delete/rename **plus** a ref
   crossing unresolved↔resolved. This is codegraph #1502's shape in our own suite.

## 4 — Make provenance travel with the value  ·  owner: graph-tech-lead

ef-manager's Q0, and the best structural result of the day. The rule form —
"state the population" — cannot work, and not for discipline reasons: **the person
stating the population is the person who already knows it.** You cannot notice an
assumption you are currently making. So the basis must be emitted by the CODE.

**We already shipped this, on exactly one number:**
`lspVerifiedPctDenominator: "verifiable_and_in_scope_calls"`.

**Generalise it: every emitted ratio carries a basis field.** Then the invalid
comparison becomes visibly invalid on the page to a reader who knows nothing about
extractor internals:

```
echoes   1.4%   basis: "no-generic-references (cpp.js:545)"
APG     14.6%   basis: "generic-identifier-references (generic.js:350)"
```

It stops being a judgement call and becomes a mismatched string.

**This is one principle with four existing rules:** *a caveat must be computed from
the same source as the number it qualifies* · *the server commit is an experimental
parameter* · *success must attest corpus and scope* · and this. All four say: **attach
provenance to the value so the value cannot travel without it.** A cross-population
error is a number that travelled into a comparison and left its denominator behind.

**Also in scope:** `trust: weak (floor)` → `trust: unknown, cause:
stale_classifier_artifact`. A floor is meaningful for a count and a category error for
an ordinal whose exclusions are the missing data. And `weak` is unfalsifiable from the
consumer side — you cannot tell "weak because the graph is bad" from "weak because the
classifier did not run". Fail-closed is a **prompt to go measure**, not a resting
state.

## 5 — The dead surface  ·  owner: graph-senior-dev-hermes + ef-manager

42 verbs, 17 listed. Audit found 25 unlisted: 12 fold/delete, 9 specialists, 4 merge.

**The reporter's limit was correct and is not overruled:** with no telemetry, "nobody
calls X" is unclaimable. But that limit gets **dissolved, not worked around**:

- **5.1 — Delete on REDUNDANCY, which is observable in source and needs no telemetry.**
  `graph_digest` explicitly composes overview/hotspots/cycles; the hidden-full comment
  already calls `change_plan`/`preflight` redundant. "This is the same answer twice" is
  a property of the code.
- **5.2 — ★ Highest value-per-line in the entire register.** Each of the 11
  self-declared-redundant verbs emits a **one-shot deprecation log on call**, naming
  its replacement. Never fires → delete it, and we now HAVE the evidence we said we
  lacked. Fires → the comment was wrong, and we learned something better than a
  deletion. **Either way the unknown resolves itself**, and it manufactures exactly the
  post-gating telemetry whose absence blocks 5.1.

**Why the eleven comments existed at all** (ef-manager's Q3, and the generalisation
worth keeping): *a comment is a note to nobody.* No owner, no date, no trigger, no
consequence. A failing test creates obligation; a runtime deprecation creates
obligation; a comment creates none. Writing them down **was the entire action taken** —
the comment discharged the feeling of having addressed it, eleven times.

**The cost principle that governs deletion here:** a hidden verb costs the consumer
zero tokens and costs the maintainer tests, docs and refactor drag forever. The cost is
not removed, it is **moved to someone who does not get a vote** — which is worse than
either party paying, because the party bearing the cost has no signal about the value
and the party who could judge the value pays nothing.

## 6 — Confidence must track coverage, in both directions  ·  owner: graph-tech-lead

The unifying principle, and the merge of two things we had been treating separately:

- **Contradiction surface** — fields that CREATE doubt you did not have. Adds
  warranted doubt.
- **Attest corpus and scope** — preventing outputs that REMOVE doubt they have not
  earned. Blocks unwarranted confidence.

Not the same axis; **opposite ends of one line.** A success report that does not attest
its corpus is not merely uninformative, it is affirmatively confidence-creating, which
is worse than silence. A too-confident success and a missing contradiction are one bug
seen from either end.

Upstream instances, all four found independently: codegraph #1502 "complete" with 0
files · codegraph #1361 lock failure → "up to date" · Understand #628 silently dropped
cross-batch edges · graphify #2520 parse holes with exit 0 · **ours**, 2.2.

⛔ **This workstream does not absorb 2.2.** A principle that swallows its most urgent
instance is precisely how eleven redundancy comments accumulated.

## 7 — Per-language quality, unblocked in the right order  ·  owner: graph-senior-dev-hermes

**The 105.3% PHP figure is RETIRED as a quality rate**, killed twice over:

1. **Numerator and denominator are different populations.** 53,197 raw per-occurrence
   unresolved attempts over 50,527 *deduplicated* materialized edges. Not
   complementary partitions of one denominator, so >100% is legal and it was never a
   rate.
2. **It is a pre-filter snapshot.** lc-api indexed 2026-04-20; the lowercase-REFERENCES
   filter landed `7fabd93` on 2026-04-21. We were quoting a number from the day before
   its own fix.

★ **The second reason is the more valuable finding: a stale artifact does not announce
staleness.** It announced 53,197 with total confidence. Third instance of that class
in three subsystems — the stale MCP process, the `unresolved-categorization.json` with
no `graph_commit`, and this.

**Retention gap, and it is a finding in itself:** the uncapped `dirty-edges.full.json`
is absent; only a 500-row file-order-capped sample survives (0.94%). A headline count
whose specimens were discarded is **unauditable by construction**.

### Order, and it does not get reordered
1. Rebuild lc-api once with the current resolver.
2. Retain an uncapped sidecar **or** write a shape histogram at index time.
3. *Only then* measure the residual import / dynamic-receiver / Laravel tails.
4. No PHP extractor work before step 3.

**Separately and orthogonally:** `laravel.js` does not parse `$middlewareGroups` /
`$routeMiddleware`, so those `PASSES_THROUGH` edges are **never emitted**. That is a
RECALL defect causing measured trace loss — absent edges cannot enter dirtyEdges, so
it cannot explain unresolved-row inflation. Two workstreams, not one.

## 8 — The cold-orientation experiment  ·  owner: ef-manager (adjudicator), graph-senior-dev-hermes (runner)

Pre-registered with falsifier and prediction, locked before data. Runner/adjudicator
split is genuine: ef-manager fixed the falsifier and cannot revise it; the runner
produces data and cannot revise the falsifier.

**Status: RAN, ruled INADMISSIBLE on capture — and then found to have a contaminated
CONSTRUCT, which is the larger defect.**

### 8.1 ★★ The contamination, and it is ours

**Our own MCP server injects an opinion about which tools matter into the system prompt
of every session carrying it.** Verbatim from the shipped `instructions` block:

> "ORIENT FIRST … `graph_health` — run ONCE at session start; nothing else answers
> 'can I trust what I am about to be told'. Field-rated the highest-value call here: it
> caught a wrong-toolchain compile DB."

Plus ~40 lines naming `graph_packet` as the "first move WHEN STILL ORIENTING", the
briefs, and tool-selection-by-intent.

So the subject was **told** to call `graph_health` first, in superlative terms, before
it saw the repo. ef-manager's registered prediction — "it will NOT call `graph_health`
unprompted" — was structurally unsatisfiable by the apparatus. First-call-is-health is
**compliance, not discovery**.

⚠ **I wrote that text.** It is not an environmental quirk; it is the always-paid
surface I spent two days optimising, contaminating an experiment I helped design. I did
not notice because I do not experience it as instructions — it is furniture in my own
prompt. Logged as another instance of section 4's principle: *the person stating the
assumption is the person already making it.*

★ **Why this was urgent rather than interesting** (ef-manager): an inadmissible run is
cheap to discard. **An admissible run measuring the wrong construct becomes evidence,
and evidence is sticky.** Rerunning with a lossless recorder and an unfixed construct
would have been strictly worse than the current state.

### 8.2 What survives — the falsifier does

The injected text names **verbs**. It names **not one of the seven fields** in the
falsifier (`overlayQuality`, `dirtySeams`, `last_touched`, `nextActions`, `trust`,
`overlay_age_warning`, `receipt.floor_cause`).

| element | status |
|---|---|
| "will not call `graph_health` unprompted" | ⛔ dead — unsatisfiable |
| prediction of reliance on packet orientation output | ⛔ dead — packet is named in the instructions |
| "leans on summary / the docs layer" | ⚠ weakened — briefs are named |
| **the falsifier: does it CITE any of the seven** | ✅ **intact** — nothing told the subject those fields exist |
| ★ the grep-bypass outcome | ✅ intact, and **stronger** — bypassing *despite* being told to orient first is a harder result than bypassing without being told |

⇒ **The decision D1 was commissioned to unblock — the seven delete verdicts — remains
answerable.**

### 8.3 Decision, made explicitly in advance: option (b), relabelled

**D1′ — "Given the tool's own guidance, does a fresh reader consume different FIELDS
than an expert lane does?"** Strictly weaker than D1. Answers the delete verdicts. Does
**not** answer whether the verb surface is discoverable by value, and must never be
reported as if it did.

Chosen over suppressing the instructions because (i) it measures the **deployed**
condition, which is what section 1 is about — sc-manager had those instructions in
context and still chose `git cat-file`; (ii) the falsifier is clean under (b) anyway;
and (iii) suppression means a different server invocation, which is perceptible to the
subject and so is a revision, not an execution detail.

⇒ **The discoverability question does not die, it moves** — to section 1, answered by
asking the two managers what they anticipated at the moment they chose grep. Better
evidence than one cold subagent, because they are the actual population.

### 8.4 Rerun constraints, if ef-manager clears D1′

- **Recorder changes, nothing else.** ef-manager's reusable criterion, adopted as a
  standing rule: *a change is an EXECUTION detail if the subject cannot perceive it,
  and a REVISION if it could alter the subject's behaviour.*
- ⛔ **If the lossless recorder means a different harness, verify the harness presents
  the same tool surface** — including that it carries aify-project-graph with the
  **same `instructions` block**. A different MCP server set is perceptible, and would
  silently convert an execution detail into a revision.
- The first run's CONTENT does not get partially adjudicated to salvage it.

## The through-line

Nineteen defects this week reduce to one shape: **a stand-in accepted for the thing
itself.** A comment for a decision. Source text for behaviour. A file-level edge for a
symbol-level claim. `vec3` for the target symbol. A pre-filter snapshot for current
state. An unread field for a useful one. A rule for a remedy.

And the fix is the same each time, which is why section 4 is the load-bearing one:
**make the artifact carry what it is a stand-in for, so it cannot travel without it.**
Rules depend on remembering. Emitted provenance does not.
