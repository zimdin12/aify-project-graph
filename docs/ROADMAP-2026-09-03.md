## 2. What the findings actually say, grouped

52 findings is not a list anyone will read. They collapse into five patterns.

### PATTERN A — Fail-open is the dominant defect class ⛔ *the through-line of the whole arc*

`FINDING-contract-failed-open`, `FINDING-results-banner-failed-open`,
`FINDING-tiers-fail-in-opposite-directions`, `FINDING-the-answer-path-cannot-refuse`, plus today's
gitignore-negation defect.

Every one has the same shape: **the tool reports itself more complete, cleaner or more confident than
it is.** Not one of them errs toward excessive doubt. That asymmetry is the single most important
fact about this codebase, because the entire product claim is "we never assert a false absence".

⇒ **This is not a backlog of unrelated bugs. It is one recurring failure mode and it attacks the only
differentiator we have.**

### PATTERN B — Things built correctly that nothing calls

`quality-of-the-unreachable` (now **four** recorded instances), `FINDING-a-check-nothing-consumes`,
`FINDING-remedy-reachability`, `FINDING-contract-reachability`,
`FINDING-m3a-debated-an-unreachable-flag`, `FINDING-never-named-is-not-unrouted`.

Today's is the sharpest: `absenceAuthority` is computed, fails closed on every clause, is tested —
and the verb that answers "who calls this" does not read it. The gate sits on `graph_health`, which
an agent calls **once at session start**, not on the verb it calls **mid-task**.

⇒ **New rule, earned:** when a gate exists, do not ask whether it is correct — ask which surface a
*hurried* reader hits, and check that one. "It is enforced in health" is not enforcement.

### PATTERN C — My instruments fail more often than the code

`verification-instrument-failures` has grown all arc. Today alone: I searched `String(object)` — the
15 characters `[object Object]` — for a field name, found nothing, and printed **"the surfaces agree,
the reviewer's point does not apply here."** A fail-open instrument whose comfortable answer arrived
first. Same run: the verdict rendered while one of its own controls printed FAIL, because I
preregistered four controls and gated on three — the second time I have shipped that mismatch.

⇒ Mechanical guards caught essentially all of it. Remembered rules caught almost none.

### PATTERN D — Freshness is answered, and mostly answered NO

`FINDING-one-edit-costs-a-full-rebuild`, `FINDING-max-wait-works-and-is-unaffordable`,
`FINDING-debounce-starves-under-continuous-editing`, `FINDING-watcher-idle-cost`,
`FINDING-watcher-misses-new-files`, `FINDING-m3b-does-not-earn-its-place`.

M3b was dropped **on measurement**, which is the system working. What remains open is small and

## 3. The strategic problem, stated plainly

The three weeks improved **the correctness of what the tool says about itself.** That is real and it
is verified.

But two findings, taken together, say that improvement has not been reaching anyone:

- The flagship safety property **was not wired into the answer path** (Pattern B, today).
- In published telemetry, **58–64% of agent sessions never invoke a query tool at all**, and our own
  skills measured 9 invocations ever, 0 from 1,059 subagent transcripts.

⇒ We have been raising the quality of an answer that is often not requested, and whose safety gate
was not attached. **Index quality has not been the binding constraint.** Reach and wiring have.

### ⭐⭐⭐ FIELD EVIDENCE, verified 2026-09-03 — an outside team has already written us down

sc-critic (Sand Castle lane, different codebase, asked to argue the target is wrong) reported that
their project's agent instructions steer agents away from us. **I checked their repo rather than
taking it on trust.** Their verbatim sentence is not in the files I read — but the posture is, and it
is worse than a paraphrase:

> `CLAUDE.md:31` — "⚠ Read its trust line first: graph-backed caller sets **cannot attest absence**"
>
> `AGENTS.md:1726` — "**1 brief read + at most 3 live verb calls per planning task. 0 live calls is
> often correct after the brief.**"

⇒ An outside team, unprompted and without our involvement, has (a) told its agents that our caller
sets **cannot attest absence** — the exact inverse of our flagship claim — and (b) **budget-capped**
us at three calls, with zero named as often correct.

⚠ And they were right before we were. Today's finding proved the absence gate was never wired into
the answer path. **They inferred from behaviour what we only just proved from the code.**

This is the strongest evidence in the whole arc, and it is about **trust and invocation**, not
precision. No amount of graph accuracy edits either line above.

### What is genuinely defensible

LSP 3.18, SCIP, LSIF, Kythe and Sourcegraph have **no field anywhere for coverage or
exhaustiveness.** None can tell an agent "I searched 11% of this repo." Our claim is therefore not
"we find more callers" — published work says we will not, recall sits ≈0.66 across all arms — it is
**"we never claim a false absence, and we state what we did not look at."**

⚠ The ceiling on that is low if measured as productivity: perfect localization buys ~3.3 points of
downstream repair, perfect exploration ~9. The investment case rests on **not taking confidently
wrong destructive actions**, and must be argued that way or not at all.

### R1 — Fix the fail-open class, starting with the two found today ⭐ HIGHEST VALUE

1. **`graph_callers` must consult the coverage gate.** Decide the shape with ef-manager — refuse
   outright / downgrade claim strength / per-symbol floor. My reversible default if they do not
   reply: **per-symbol**, following `evidence.exhaustive`, because a repo-wide floor punishes every
   symbol for the coverage of files unrelated to it. Reserve an outright refusal for a zero spine.
2. **The gitignore evaluator must honour negations.** `.gitignore:11` is `!docs/evidence/suite/*.log`.
   Git honours it; `pathContainsIgnoredDir` does not, so a tracked modified file renders `dirty=0`.
   Per that function's own header, *every surface reporting a dirty count routes through here.*
   Population measured: 1487 tracked files, 30 genuinely shadowed, plus every negated path.
3. **Then sweep for the pattern** rather than fixing two instances. Five findings share this shape;
   a sweep is warranted and a one-off fix is not (`vacuous-truth-and-custody`: one fix is not a sweep).

### R2 — Ask the outside, before spending more ⭐ FREE, ALREADY IN FLIGHT

Sent today to **ef-manager** and **sc-critic** (whose standing lane is arguing the target is wrong,
and who works on a different codebase — a genuinely different substrate, not a second reader of
mine). Both asked to argue AGAINST, with "you should stop" named as an acceptable answer.

The question I most want back is field evidence I cannot generate from inside: *when you hit "who
calls this / is it safe to delete", what do you actually do, and would a tool that sometimes refuses
have helped or annoyed you?*

### R3 — Then run M5, on a version that delivers its own headline property

12 runs = 6 paired cells (Steven's budget). ⚠ **Do not run it before R1.** Measuring the current
build would measure a version whose safety gate is not attached to the answer.

`PREREGISTRATION-linkage-runner.md` AMENDMENT 2 stands: 4-of-6 and 5-of-6 are indistinguishable from
chance and must be reported as a null.

### R4 — ⭐ PROMOTED: measure WHY agents do not call the tool

**Was last and conditional. The verified field evidence moves it up, and sc-critic's ranking says
the same thing independently:** *"most likely the target itself — answer precision on a
near-saturated localization axis — is wrong, and the real bottleneck is invocation and trust."*

The 58–64% non-invocation number has **four different causes with four different fixes**:
discoverability, trust, latency, genuine non-need. We do not know which one we have, and precision
work only becomes rational again once the answer comes back "genuine non-need is not it."

⚠ We now have one datum and it points at **trust**: an outside team wrote "cannot attest absence"
into its instructions and capped us at three calls. That is not a discoverability problem — they
found us, evaluated us, and wrote a rule limiting us.

Cheapest first step, and it costs nothing: ask the teams that already run us why their docs say what
they say. `sand_castle` wrote those two lines for a reason and can be asked directly.
