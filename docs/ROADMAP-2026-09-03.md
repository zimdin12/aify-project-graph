`FINDING-contract-failed-open`, `FINDING-results-banner-failed-open`,
`FINDING-tiers-fail-in-opposite-directions`, `FINDING-the-answer-path-cannot-refuse`, plus today's

Every one has the same shape: **the tool reports itself more complete, cleaner or more confident than

⇒ **This is not a backlog of unrelated bugs. It is one recurring failure mode and it attacks the only

### PATTERN B — Things built correctly that nothing calls

## 3. The strategic problem, stated plainly

The three weeks improved **the correctness of what the tool says about itself.** That is real and it
is verified.

But two findings, taken together, say that improvement has not been reaching anyone:

- The flagship safety property **was not wired into the answer path** (Pattern B, today).
- In published telemetry, **58–64% of agent sessions never invoke a query tool at all**, and our own
  skills measured 9 invocations ever, 0 from 1,059 subagent transcripts.

⇒ We have been raising the quality of an answer that is often not requested, and whose safety gate
was not attached. **Index quality has not been the binding constraint.** Reach and wiring have.

sc-critic (Sand Castle lane, different codebase, asked to argue the target is wrong) reported that
their project's agent instructions steer agents away from us.

⚠ **AND THE THIRD LINE IS A BELIEF, NOT AN INCIDENT.** I asked what produced it. sc-critic traced

⚠ And they were right before we were. Today's finding proved the absence gate was never wired into
the answer path. **They inferred from behaviour what we only just proved from the code** — and wrote
a positive-control requirement around us that this project's own rules would endorse.

This is the strongest evidence in the whole arc, and it is about **trust and invocation**, not
precision. No amount of graph accuracy edits either line above.

### ⭐⭐⭐ WE CANNOT ATTEST ABSENCE BY CONSTRUCTION — verified in code, 2026-09-03

ef-manager grepped the SKILLS layer rather than the verb, which is where the honest answer had been
sitting for two weeks. Verified verbatim in this checkout:

> `integrations/claude-code/skills/cpp-inner-loop/SKILL.md:69-70` — "For 'is this dead / safe to
> delete', verify with `rg` — that is not a fallback, **it is currently the only sound method**."

The doc says "as of 2026-08-19", and a blocker expires with its measurement, so I checked the code
rather than trust the date. It is not a withholding pending a fix:

> `mcp/stdio/query/verbs/code_intel_live.js:305-318` — on the **success** path
> (`freshness === 'fresh' && callsiteCount > 0`): `exhaustive: false`, `completeness: 'floor'`,
> `fallback: 'each returned location is compiler-resolved; for absence, verify with rg'`
>
> `mcp/stdio/query/cause-classification.js:24` — "`index_population_unattested` is true of **EVERY
> call** — the compile DB never reports which TUs clangd actually indexed."

⇒ `exhaustive` can never become true on this architecture. The compile DB selects which files the
language server MAY index and never reports which it DID. **The flagship claim is unsupportable by
construction, and our own code has said so on every call since 2026-08-19.**

⚠ **THIS CORRECTS THE SECTION ABOVE.** I recorded sand_castle's "cannot attest absence — grep is the
instrument" as a BELIEF with no incident behind it, and called it an unearned reputation. It is
neither unearned nor a belief: it is an accurate reading of our own published position. They were
repeating our documentation back to us. The adoption lesson survives; the framing was wrong.

⇒ And it reframes today's other finding. Wiring `absenceAuthority` into `graph_callers` would NOT
restore the absence claim — it would only make the refusal honest. Ship it as a **bug fix**, never as
the product bet.

### What survives, and it is not nothing

⛔ Do not read the above as "stop". Both reviewers pushed back on undersell, and they are right that
two capabilities are structurally beyond grep and **do not depend on exhaustiveness at all**:

- **TRANSITIVITY** — "what breaks across N hops". grep does one hop and makes the agent drive the
  recursion, paying context per hop.
- **VIRTUAL DISPATCH / OVERRIDES** — grep cannot resolve `base*->virt()`. Structural, not a quality gap.

⇒ **Drop "safe to delete" from the pitch rather than fixing it.** It is our weakest surface, it is
where rg is strongest, it is where being wrong is most expensive, and our own skill already
surrendered it. Build the pitch — and the A/B — on transitivity and virtual dispatch.

⚠ Note what that does to the caveat-cut question I twice refused to answer: the coverage disclosure
exists mostly to service the claim being dropped. Drop the claim and the question dissolves rather
than getting answered.

### What is genuinely defensible

LSP 3.18, SCIP, LSIF, Kythe and Sourcegraph have **no field anywhere for coverage or
exhaustiveness.** None can tell an agent "I searched 11% of this repo." Our claim is therefore not
"we find more callers" — published work says we will not, recall sits ≈0.66 across all arms — it is
**"we never claim a false absence, and we state what we did not look at."**

⚠ The ceiling on that is low if measured as productivity: perfect localization buys ~3.3 points of
downstream repair, perfect exploration ~9. The investment case rests on **not taking confidently
wrong destructive actions**, and must be argued that way or not at all.

## 5. What is Steven's to decide

1. **After R1, run the 12-run A/B — or call the stop condition now?** His preregistered condition:
   if M1 and M2 ship and a graph-armed agent still cannot beat the same agent with grep, the honest
   conclusion is that the value is orientation and structure only. M1 and M2 have shipped. **We are
   at that decision point.**

   ⛔ **AND THE CONDITION AS WRITTEN CLAIMS MORE THAN 12 RUNS CAN PAY — his call to accept, made
   before the runs, not after.** Six paired cells with a 5-of-6 bar has this power, computed
   2026-09-04 and checked rather than quoted:

   | true win rate of the graph arm | P(clears 5 of 6) |
   |---|---|
   | 0.50 — a coin | **10.9%** (7/64) |
   | 0.70 — helps clearly and often | **42.0%** |
   | 0.90 — obviously helps | **88.6%** |

   So the design separates *obviously helps* from *coin*, and **cannot** separate *helps at a
   commercially real rate* from *does nothing*: a genuinely useful 70%-win tool returns our NULL
   **58% of the time**. ⇒ A null at this scale licenses **"no LARGE effect detected, and this design
   cannot distinguish a moderate real effect from none"**. It does **not** license "our value is
   orientation and structure only". Reading it as the strong conclusion would be the pilot error run
   backwards — there I explained away an unflattering result; here I would be over-reading one.
   ⇒ Recommendation: **run it, and report the bound with the result.** A cheap test that rules out a
   large effect is worth having, as long as nobody reads its null as ruling out a moderate one.
   Raising the cell count to buy power is not proposed — 12 is his ceiling, and the honest response
   to low power is to report the interval, not to spend money he capped.
2. **Is "never claims a false absence" a product he wants to sell**, given the productivity ceiling
   is ~3 points and the real claim is about avoided destructive actions?
3. **Whether adoption (R4) is this project's problem at all**, or belongs to the harness/skills layer.
