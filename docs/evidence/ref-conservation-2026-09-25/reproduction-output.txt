COST OF THE FIX, measured rather than estimated
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

closure-cost.py, on this repository's own graph (1934 files with nodes, 914 of them code),
40 code files sampled, seed 20260926 — one-level expansion against the fixed point:

  one-level:   median 2, mean 5.2, max 33
  fixed-point: median 4, mean 373.0, max 1047      (54.1% of all files with nodes)
  files where the closure adds NOTHING: 20 of 40

So the distribution is bimodal: half of all edits cost nothing extra, and an edit to a file deep in
the import graph pulls in roughly half the repository.

Wall clock in a disposable worktree, same machine, same commit:

  FULL REBUILD (force)                              48,935 ms
  INCREMENTAL of a hub file WITH the closure        50,799 ms

⇒ THE WORST CASE IS BOUNDED BY WHAT A FULL REBUILD COSTS ANYWAY. A hub-file edit goes from roughly
  8.6s (the post-commit reindex times in .aify-graph/hook.log) to ~50s; a median edit is unchanged.
  Paying a full rebuild's cost on hub edits is the price of not silently deleting edges that license
  a delete.

⚠ A CHEAPER DESIGN EXISTS AND IS NOT BUILT. Instead of re-extracting every transitive owner, capture
  the incoming edges before the cascade and RESTORE the ones whose target still exists afterwards,
  writing an unresolved_refs row for the ones whose target genuinely vanished. That is O(incoming
  edges) rather than O(transitive closure), and it would also make the vanishing case loud instead of
  silent. It is recorded here rather than built because it needs the per-file node pipeline to expose
  a post-flush hook, and shipping the correct-but-slow version first is the safer order.

⛔ AND THE HALF THIS FIX DOES NOT ADDRESS: every graph built before it is already missing edges, and
  such a graph cannot tell you so — the loss is self-concealing. Only a full rebuild repairs one, and
  nothing marks one as needing that. A possibly-lossy flag, persisted on the committed generation and
  cleared only by a full rebuild, with a missing flag reading UNKNOWN rather than clean, is the
  outstanding piece. Raised by dashboard-manager; the legacy gate was independently flagged by
  graph-senior-dev.
